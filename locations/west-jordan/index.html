Two Eras of Homes, One Standard of Clean

West Jordan is unusual in Utah County in that it has real housing diversity — 1970s brick ranches near the Bangerter Highway corridor, mid-era tract homes through the Jordan River neighborhoods, and newer construction pushing toward the Mountain View Corridor. Each era brings its own window challenges.

Older homes often have original frames that have absorbed years of oxidation, mineral haze from hard water irrigation, and Oquirrh Mountain dust blown in from the west. Newer Copper Hills builds face a different problem — modern window packages are larger and often installed with construction residue still on the glass. We handle both.

The Jordan River Parkway makes West Jordan a genuinely pleasant place, but proximity to water means faster mineral and particulate buildup on glass. Homes backing the Jordan River trail typically benefit from two to three cleans per year to stay ahead of it.

West Jordan Window Facts

Oquirrh Mountain dust blows eastward toward the Jordan River corridor — west- and north-facing glass accumulates grime faster than south-facing windows.

Older 1970s–80s brick homes near Bangerter often have original aluminum frames that benefit from careful technique to avoid scratching aged seals.

Copper Hills and Mountain View Corridor builds have larger window packages — more glass per home means exterior-only cleans take longer, but the difference is visible across the entire front elevation.

Utah's hard water leaves calcium and silica deposits that become permanent if left too long. Annual cleaning keeps mineral buildup from etching into the glass permanently.
