## Highland's Views Are Worth Protecting

Highland sits above the Utah Valley floor, which gives its homes some of the most dramatic unobstructed views of Mount Timpanogos and the Wasatch Range in the county. Those views are a big part of what drew people to the city — and they're exactly what gets blocked when elevated windows go uncleaned through a full season.

The elevation advantage that makes the views possible also creates a more aggressive soiling environment. Without the natural windbreak of buildings and tree cover lower in the valley, Highland's west-facing windows take the full force of seasonal dust storms rolling across the valley floor. Spring brings valley-floor pollen and topsoil particulate; late summer adds wildfire smoke haze from canyons to the east and south. The result is a layered film that can make clean glass look permanently dull between professional cleanings.

Do elevated Highland homes need more frequent window cleaning?

Yes. Highland's elevation above the valley floor means more consistent wind exposure, particularly from the west and south. West-facing windows accumulate dust, pollen, and particulate faster than in lower-elevation cities. Most Highland homeowners benefit from at least two full cleanings per year — homes near the ridgeline often need three.

What causes the hazy film on Highland's west-facing windows?

The open western exposure means valley-floor dust during dry spring and fall months, combined with wildfire smoke in late summer, creates a layered film that builds up faster than in more sheltered locations. Standard wiping doesn't fully clear it — professional cleaning with the right solution removes every layer.

Do you serve the hillside neighborhoods near the Highland and Alpine border?

Yes — we serve all of Highland, including properties along the Highland/Alpine border, Stone Willow, The Crossing, and homes up the bench toward Lone Peak. We're experienced with elevated access, custom home layouts, and large-format glass on multi-story properties.

Does hard water from Wasatch snowmelt affect Highland windows?

Yes. Highland's water supply draws from Wasatch snowmelt, which is mineral-heavy. Sprinkler systems deposit calcium and magnesium on glass throughout the irrigation season. Left untreated long enough, those deposits etch into the surface. Professional cleaning removes current buildup before it becomes permanent damage.
