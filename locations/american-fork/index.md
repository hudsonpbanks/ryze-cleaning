## Why American Fork Windows Need More Attention Than Most

American Fork sits at the base of one of Utah County's most-traveled canyon corridors. The same Wasatch snowmelt that supplies the city's water carries calcium and magnesium through pipes, sprinkler systems, and rain runoff. Every sprinkler hit leaves a trace of mineral deposit on your glass. Over enough seasons, those deposits don't wipe off — they need the right solution and technique to clear without scratching.

Late summer adds a different problem. Wildfire smoke from the canyons and surrounding range settles as fine gray particulate on north and west-facing glass. It's not always visible until the light catches it right — but it dulls every view from inside the house. A post-smoke cleaning before the cold sets in is the most consistent request we get from American Fork homeowners, and it's one of the highest-impact cleanings of the year.

How often should American Fork homes get their windows cleaned?

Twice a year at minimum — once in late spring after pollen and snowmelt season, and again in early fall after wildfire smoke season. Homes adjacent to American Fork Canyon or with active sprinkler systems may benefit from a third mid-summer cleaning.

Does hard water from the Wasatch really affect my windows?

Yes. The snowmelt that supplies American Fork's water is mineral-heavy. Sprinkler overspray and rain leave calcium and magnesium deposits on glass. Left untreated long enough, those deposits etch into the surface. Professional cleaning removes current buildup before it becomes permanent damage.

What is the difference between exterior-only and the full package?

Exterior cleaning handles all outside-facing glass, frames, and sills. The full interior and exterior package adds all interior glass — removing smudges, pet prints, and indoor haze. Most customers book the full package once or twice a year and exterior-only for additional seasonal cleanings. Booking both together costs less than booking separately.
