## At the Canyon Mouth — Windows Take More of a Beating Here

Spanish Fork sits where Spanish Fork Canyon meets the valley floor. That geography is part of what makes the city great — easy access to US-6, the canyon, and the scenery — but it also means the wind has a direct path from the mountains into your neighborhood. Canyon winds funnel fine dust, pollen, and agricultural particulate from the surrounding valley floor directly into the city. East-facing windows on homes near the canyon mouth accumulate debris faster than comparable homes anywhere else in Utah County.

Irrigation from nearby farmland adds another layer: water with a high mineral content hits glass through sprinkler drift or runoff, leaves a residue, and evaporates — repeating that cycle every day through the summer. By September, windows that haven't been cleaned since spring often have visible white scaling on glass and frames.
