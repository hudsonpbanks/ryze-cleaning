## Why Payson Windows Accumulate Debris Year-Round

Payson sits at the southern end of Utah Valley where the valley floor transitions to foothill terrain, and the city is surrounded by active agricultural land, orchards, and open fields on multiple sides. That agricultural character is central to what makes Payson distinctive — and it's also what creates a persistent and varied debris load on glass throughout the year.

Spring in Payson means orchard bloom season, when pollen from cherry, peach, and apple orchards in and around the city fills the air. This fine, sticky pollen bonds to glass quickly and can be challenging to remove without the right approach. As the growing season progresses, tilling cycles on adjacent fields kick up soil dust that settles on windows in nearby neighborhoods. Late summer harvest activity produces its own particulate that extends the heavy-deposit season through early fall.

Payson's mix of older established homes and newer east bench construction means we adjust our approach based on the property. Older homes may have original frames with deeper tracks that accumulate years of debris, while newer builds often have modern windows but may still show the effects of nearby agricultural activity.
